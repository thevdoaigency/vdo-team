AWC Film Crew SOP v1.0 — 2026
Freelance Camera
& Sound Crew
All Portfolio Projects

This SOP applies to every freelance cinematographer, camera operator, and sound recordist contracted for an AWC portfolio project. Read it before your first shoot. The companion Jobsite Guide covers on-site conduct, safety, and shot list.

00 / Who's Who

Roles

Everyone contracted to an AWC project falls into one of these roles. Small crews often stack them (one person = cinematographer + recordist). The responsibilities do not stack down.

Field

Cinematographer / DP

Owns visual capture. Responsible for camera settings, LUT delivery, footage organization, and on-site conduct per the Jobsite Guide.

Field

Camera Operator (B / C)

Reports to the cinematographer. Matches settings and format. Delivers footage into the correct camera folder.

Field

Sound Recordist

Owns audio capture. Responsible for two-source recording, sound file organization, and sync references. Doubles as a plant mic rigger.

Post

Media Manager

Receives all footage and audio on behalf of AWC. A shoot is not complete until the media manager confirms receipt.

01 / Before the Shoot

Pre-Production

Confirm all of the following in the week before your call time.

  • Call sheet received from production
  • Site address, superintendent name, and access instructions confirmed
  • PPE ready or confirmed provided on site
  • Gear list shared with production
  • Jobsite Guide reviewed
  • Media manager contact confirmed
  • Backup drive on hand with at least 2x the expected footage capacity
  • MASV or approved transfer service access ready
02 / Image

Camera Standards

All footage is captured for a color pipeline. Deliverables must round-trip cleanly to the media manager without guesswork.

Codec
RAW or Log/Flat. S-Log3, N-Log, C-Log, V-Log, ProRes RAW, BRAW all acceptable.
Resolution
4K minimum. UHD or DCI both fine.
Frame Rate
24fps standard. 60fps for motion intended for slow-motion.
Color / LUT
Deliver EITHER (a) a custom LUT built to your camera and lighting for that shoot, OR (b) the manufacturer LUT name so the media manager applies the correct conversion. No LUT = incomplete delivery.
White Balance
Locked. Never auto.
Field Guide
Full camera settings (shutter, ISO, ND, padding) per the Jobsite Guide for Film Crew.
03 / Sound

Sound Standards

Two independent sources on every recorded human. Always. If the primary fails, the secondary is what saves the interview.

Primary — body-worn

  • DJI Mic (or DJI Mic 2)
  • Rode Wireless (any Pro model)
  • TASCAM DR-10L or DR-10L Pro
  • Sennheiser or Sony wireless lav pack

Secondary — boom, plant, or backup

  • Boom mic on a pole (shotgun)
  • Plant mic hidden in the scene
  • Second lav on the same subject
  • An iPhone with Voice Memos or the Rode app running quietly as an emergency backup — acceptable as the secondary source, never as the primary
Any interview subject or follow footage where the subject is narrating must be recorded with a body-worn mic. The secondary source is a rule, not a suggestion.
Format
48 kHz / 24-bit WAV minimum
Sync
Head slate on every clip if crew allows. Otherwise a clap or a hand-signed sync mark on camera.
Levels
Peaks around -12 dB. Never let it clip.
04 / Organization

File Naming

Every shoot day gets its own top-level folder from each crew member. Names are locked to this format so the media manager can find anything in seconds.

Cinematographer folder

FormatYYMMDD_Cinematographer_Name
Example260722_Cinematographer_JohnDoe
260722_Cinematographer_JohnDoe/ ├── A_CAM/ // primary camera clips ├── B_CAM/ // second camera clips └── C_CAM/ // third camera or drone (if used)

Only include folders for cameras that actually rolled. Do not deliver empty folders.

Sound recordist folder

FormatYYMMDD_Recordist_Name
Example260722_Recordist_JaneSmith
260722_Recordist_JaneSmith/ └── all sound files inside. Sub-folder by session if useful.

If a cinematographer is also handling sound, sound files go inside a Recordist folder alongside the Cinematographer folder — not mixed in with camera folders.

05 / Handoff

Delivery

Footage must land in the media manager's approved cloud inbox. Which service you use is flexible — the standard is professional, tracked, and secure.

Approved
  • MASV — AWC account (request access from media manager)
  • WeTransfer Pro
  • Aspera
  • Signiant
  • Frame.io Transfer
  • Dropbox Transfer (paid tier)
  • Any other professional file-transfer service
Not Accepted
  • Google Drive of any kind
  • iCloud shared folders
  • Consumer free-tier services with expiring links
  • Physical drive hand-off with no cloud copy
  • Slack, email, or SMS attachments
Footage delivered via Google Drive will not be counted as delivered. This is a hard rule.
06 / Retention

Backup & 60-Day Hold

  1. Camera and sound crews hold a full backup on their own drive for 60 days from the shoot date.
  2. After day 60, the backup may be purged unless AWC has asked in writing for an extended hold.
  3. Card originals stay untouched until the delivered copy is confirmed received by the media manager AND the local backup is verified.
  4. If AWC needs a re-pull, the request will come before day 60. Respond within 48 hours.
07 / Sign-Off

What Counts as Delivered

A shoot is considered delivered — and the job is closed — when all five of these are true.

  • Footage and audio have landed in the media manager's approved cloud inbox
  • Folder structure matches the naming convention above
  • LUT (custom or manufacturer name) has been provided
  • 60-day local backup is in place and verified
  • Media manager has confirmed receipt in writing

Anything less and the shoot remains open. Sign-off waits on delivery confirmation.
